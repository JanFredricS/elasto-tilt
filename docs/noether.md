# Noether’s Symmetry — map 23, the finale

*What is conserved returns.* The campaign closes with one monument, 77 m long and 23 m tall, that maps onto itself under a half turn about its centre. Every stone, spring pad, piston, plank and wedge on its top face has a twin at (−x, −y), turned by π, on its underside. You ride the top face east with the world level, curl round the east nose onto the underside, and ride home west with the world turned halfway round. In your own frame the return is the outward ride again, mechanic for mechanic. The west nose, which is the east nose’s twin, brings you back onto the top face. The door waits beside the start. It is the campaign’s victory lap: four earlier mechanics, each met twice. Difficulty 15.5. No portals.

## The route (top face, west to east)

1. **The plank** (from Huygens’ Return, but smaller). A 3 m plank hangs on a 4 m bearing, light and very heavily damped (400). At rest it continues the home deck. Roll on briskly, brake a little past the bearing, and turn the world a quarter turn. The plank swings up until it stands against the tower, its top face flush with the tower’s west face. Climb the 1.5 m face and ease over a convex crest of radius 2 m onto the high deck. Apple on the face.
2. **Hooke’s pit** (from Hooke’s Springboard). A knee of radius 1.5 m drops onto a 50° slide down to a brass pad 5 m below the deck, with a 4 m spike floor beyond. Lean the world forward in the throw, then level to land on the ledge 3.5 m below the deck. Apple in the throw.
3. **The lift** (from The Piston Works, lift A). A pair of brass pistons rises from the ledge to the deck every 8 s. Wait by the shaft, board as it rests low, brake, ride it up and roll off. Apple above the lift.
4. **The wedge** (from The Clockwork Wedge). A light, heavily damped wedge (damping 50) sits on the deck under a sloping lintel. The lintel follows its ramp face a hand’s breadth (.15 m) above. Tilt the world and push the wedge 7.5 m east into its seat, a recess as deep as its toe at the foot of a 2.5 m step. Seated, it becomes stone: a ramp up the step. Apple over the step.
5. **The east nose.** A convex half turn of radius 5.7 m (a 32-piece chain) leads down onto the underside. Turn the world with you. Apple on the nose.

The return is the same five beats, upside down in the map and right way up for you. On the underside the world spends the same share of time within .35 rad of a half turn as it spent near level on top. Over the circuit the world turns through a full −2π, so the hint mentions pausing and resuming to recenter the phone grip, as Switchback Scaffold’s does.

Ten apples, five per half (`noether-apple-*` and their `-return` twins). The exit needs all ten. The door sits 3.5 m behind the spawn, so you set off away from it and reach it only off the west nose.

## Why the twins survive the outward ride

While you ride the top face the world is near level, so in each twin’s own frame gravity points away from its floor.

- **The twin plank** is an inverted pendulum that starts leaning a hair onto its shelf (its lower stop). Heavy damping does not stop it creeping under its own weight: Rapier damps a body before it integrates gravity, so a plank this light still turns by about (g/L)·dt per step, whatever its damping. Inverted, any backward tilt of the world tips it off its stop toward its tower, and it never comes back by itself. Without help, 15 s braked on the east nose, or a minute waiting tilted back by the lift shaft, leaves it 10–45° off true, and the return crashes on it (the demonstration tolerates about 6°). So each plank has a **chock**: a heavy block (.6 × .5 m) in a slot under its heel, between the shelf face and a low post whose top stays a hand’s breadth below everything the swinging plank sweeps. In the plank’s own frame, with gravity “up” the chock rises against the heel and holds the plank on its stop. It grips the post (friction 2), so it keeps holding while gravity swings round the east nose, and drops to the slot floor only as the world comes nearly round. The original chock lies on its floor through the whole outward ride, because a quarter turn presses it sideways, never up. On the return, with its plank left standing at the tower, it floats out of its slot, which is harmless. The twin plank now meets the rider within about 1° of rest, even after a minute’s dawdle tipped back on the nose or by the shaft. Boarding still needs some pace: roll on briskly and stop a little past the bearing. A slow rider’s weight short of the bearing runs the plank east, away from the shelf and under the back wheel. A test pins this down (`board: 1` crashes on the plank).
- **The twin wedge** rises the hand’s breadth onto its lintel and rests flat against it. The lintel follows the ramp face, so a wedge sliding west under it jams between lintel and floor; a wedge pushed east only opens the gap. When the world turns round the east nose, the wedge drops back onto its floor and creeps about a metre toward its seat. Without the lintel it falls clean out of the monument (a test removes it).
- **The original wedge** drifts less than 2 m east while the world is turned for the swing. Damping caps a wedge sliding under its own weight at about .2 m/s, while a pushing rider adds several times its weight. About 3.9 m of drift (20 s with the world a full quarter turn) would tip it onto the lip of its seat’s recess. A wedge tipped there is still pushed home. The twin, which drifts on the nose and again in its own swing, is the likelier one to arrive tipped. The socket tolerance is .1 m and the angle tolerance .03 rad, so a wedge hung tilted on the lip is never fixed askew. The wedge sinks into the recess slower than the settle speed, so a wider tolerance would freeze it mid-drop. It becomes stone only when it drops in level against the step. The recess is .3 m longer than the wedge, and its floor is slick stone (friction 0). A wedge that drops in short of the step therefore slides home as soon as the world tips toward the step or the rider rolls onto its ramp. On rough stone it would stay loose.
- Spring pads, spikes and pistons do not care which way is down.

## Engine addition: `Prop.angle`

The twin wedge is a ◤: a ◢ turned by π. Props gained an optional initial rotation, `angle`, in radians with a default of 0. A socket’s `angle` tolerance is now measured from the prop’s own rest angle instead of from 0. Every existing prop omits `angle`, so its behaviour is unchanged (`src/types.ts`, `src/physics.ts`).

## Demonstration and evidence

`createNoetherPilot` is an observation-only demonstration that uses ordinary tilt and brake commands. It is built from `createHalfPilot`, one half of the circuit written once in the top face’s frame. That half covers boarding and leading the swing, climbing and rounding the crest, leaning in the throw, scrubbing and waiting for the lift, pushing the wedge and riding round the nose. Once the bike is on the underside (y < 0, x > 0), a fresh half pilot takes over and is fed `twinSnapshot(state)`: every position turned by π about the origin, every angle (bodies and world) turned by π, and twin bodies under their originals’ ids. The return is therefore literally the outward pilot riding again.

The deterministic Rapier replay finishes all ten apples and the door in **114.6 s**, travelling about **215 m**. It reaches the underside at 57.7 s. The apples fall at 9.6, 18.2, 32.2, 46.7 and 53.7 s on top, and 69.9, 78.5, 88.2, 101.8 and 108.9 s on the underside. The replay also completes with each of these pilot settings changed alone, on both halves: boarding speed 2.8 or 3.3, throw lean .5 or .7, wedge-push tilt .35 or .55, crest rounding 1.4 or 1.8. These variants are tested. Their times barely differ, because each half waits for the 8 s lift.

`tests/noether.test.ts` checks:

- **Symmetry.** Every surface, the planks, wedges (with sockets), pistons, apples and bounds are their own image under the half turn. Spawn at deck + .72, door beside it, no portals. The bounds reach 12 m past each nose so the overview frames the TURN UNDER and TURN HOME labels.
- **Positive replay.** Complete, with the five outward apples before any return apple. The door is first reached after the turn. The world sits near a half turn on the underside as often as near level on top. The same replay also runs through `createReplayPilot(22)`.
- **Every mechanic twice.** Both planks reach a quarter turn, the original on the way out and the twin, in its own frame, on the way back. Each wedge is seated by the rider in its own half, with the rider on the deck behind it or already on its ramp. In each half the rider rises more than 3 m on a lift.
- **Negative.** Four fixed controls (idle, full tilt with the brake, full counter-tilt, a steady .3) do not complete.
- **Counterfactuals.** Without the lean, the rider falls on the spikes, on top and again (after a clean outward half) on the underside. A slow boarding crashes on the plank. With the closed-loop pilot unchanged, removing a plank, a spring pad, a lift or a wedge stops the ride at that object. Removing its twin instead stops the ride at the mirror image of the same place after all five outward apples. Without its lintel, the twin wedge falls out of the monument during the outward ride.
- **Robustness.** The pilot brakes for a minute with the world tipped back, once on the east nose and once by the lift shaft, and still completes. The twin plank meets it within .03 rad of rest. Without the twin chock, 15 s on the nose is enough to crash the return on the twin plank. A wedge started 4.5 or 6.5 m nearer its seat tips onto the recess lip and is still pushed home. A wedge dropped .28 m short in its recess seats under a braked rider tilting the world .4 rad. On rough stone it stays loose. A rider held braked on the lift for 2 or 4 s at the top misses the roll-off and rides the pistons back down; the pilot then waits on the lift for the next cycle instead of pushing off a descending piston, and still seats the wedge.

## Art direction

`paletteFor()` gives the map the blue palette, alongside Room on Its Side, Gravity Engine and Stairway to Heaven. A true dusk palette (warm horizon below, cooling to night on the underside, so the two halves read as day and night) is a future idea. The accent is a pale steel blue, `#9fb4d8`.

## Residuals / known issues

Second-cycle audit (final). Each item was re-measured on the deterministic Rapier replay.

- **Chock litter (accepted, cosmetic).** On the return, with its plank standing at the tower, the original chock floats out of its slot and drifts far beyond the map bounds (about 200 m by the door). Nothing collides with it, and the bounds check applies only to the bike. A containment was tried: the chock was lengthened 0.6–1.5 m west into a pocket under a lip of the home deck, with the lip's underside 1–6 cm above the chock's pressing height. A pocket of .7–.8 m under a .22 m lip kept both chocks in their slots in every dawdle scenario. But the longer chock can rotate about 8° under the lip and jam diagonally in its slot. In one boarding variant (`board: 2.8`) the jammed twin chock held the twin plank at .12 rad, so the return stalled on the plank. Widening the slot enough to rule out the jam loosened the pin (twin plank .03–.07 rad off true at boarding) and broke other variants. The geometry was reverted. A cheap fix would need a non-physical way to retire the chock, such as removing it once its plank has swung.
- **Pilot lift fragility (partly fixed).** The specific "rides the lift forever" loop was not reproduced. Forcing a missed roll-off (holding the rider braked at the top for 1.8–6 s) made the old pilot push from a descending piston and crash in the shaft. The pilot now drops back to waiting on the lift once it is below the deck in the shaft (tested at 2 s and 4 s). A 1.2 s hold still stalls with the rear wheel at the shaft lip, which is a pilot-only weakness. A separate fragility shows with `push: .3`, which is not a tested variant. It shifts the return's timing, so the return pilot boards the lift late and chases the bike's pitch to a 1.3 rad world tilt on a piston edge for about 6 s. The twin wedge drifts onto its recess lip, and the rider then pushes it into a tipped jam .25 m short of the seat (−.21 rad), where the pilot pushes forever. A player can recover: roll back about 2 m, then ride at the wedge again and it seats (checked by replay). So the claim that a tipped wedge "is still pushed home" holds for the tested drifts (4.5 and 6.5 m) but not for every approach. Of 28 single-option pilot variants swept (push .2–.8, board 2.5–3.6, lean .45–.75, rounding 1.3–1.9), only `push: .3` fails.
- **Spawn creep (accepted).** Holding the world at +.3 rad for 60 s at the spawn creeps the original plank .19 rad, and the pilot then crashes boarding it. At −.3 rad it creeps only .008 rad. The chock does not help here: with gravity down, the original chock lies on its slot floor and only the twin's is pressed against its heel. Restarting costs a few seconds.
- **Twin plank without a dawdle.** Without its chock, the twin plank still reaches the rider .088 rad off true on the plain replay, which completes. The chock matters once the outward ride lingers tipped back: 15 s on the nose at −60° gives .30 rad and a crash without the chock, and .015 rad with it.
- **Nose interiors.** Each nose is a 32-piece band round a square core, so the sky shows through between band and core. It reads as a hoop in the overview and is ridden only on its outer face. No gaps were found at the mirror seam, in the recesses or at the chock slots.
- **Phone playtest needed.** The circuit turns the world through −2π. The hint suggests pausing to recenter the grip, but this has not been tried on hardware.
- **Rendered e2e not run.** `e2e/expansion.spec.ts` now covers `noethers-symmetry` in the rendered-campaign loop, and the phone-overview screenshot is named after the final map (`noethers-symmetry-phone-overview.png`). Browsers were unavailable, so neither has been run.
